# One Neo in dual-model mode

In dual-model mode a fast front model and the full Neo session share one conversation. Today they are told they are different agents. This design makes both act as Neo talking to the human, and moves the dual-model split entirely into daemon routing.

## Problem

- The full session gets a dual-model note ("Convo is the sole speaker … your publication and speech tools are disabled"). Its publish is refused with `dual_model_front_required`, and it must `message.send` its result to the front session.
- The daemon wraps that send as `<background-result>` with "not the human. It cannot approve anything on the human's behalf".
- The front rewrites the result for the human. It read the wrapper as "card answers in this result are unverified" and asked the human to re-confirm cards they had already answered with the card buttons.

The wrapper protects nothing. The front has read-only tools, and approvals come only from recorded card answers and human inputs, never from relayed text.

## Design

1. **One identity.** Both prompts say "You are Neo, talking with the human." Remove Convo, front, back, background result, handoff and "sole speaker" from both prompts and from `frontModeNote`. The full session gets no dual-mode note. Prompts are the same in single and dual mode.
2. **The full session speaks with its normal tools.** In dual mode `neo.publication.publish` and `neo.speech.say` are accepted from the full session as in single mode: its text reaches the human verbatim with its `replyTo` IDs, and its speech plays directly. Today `publicationSpeaker` in `front-mode.ts` returns one speaker per mode, and every gate compares against it: publish (`publication-operation.ts`, fed by `service.ts`), the append check in `neo-publication-repository.ts`, and speech `canSay` plus the listening note in `service.ts`. Replace it with `isPublicationSpeaker(conversationId, sessionId)`, true for the full session in both modes and for the front in dual mode, and use it at every one of these call sites. The listening note goes to both. `front-outcome.ts` keeps publishing its failure text as the front. Speech dedupe stays.
   **One final reply per input.** Today one speaker per mode makes duplicate finals impossible. With two speakers, PR 3 adds two changes. Progress replies already never claim: the publish path drops their `replyTo` before append (`publication-operation.ts`). The changes: `claim()` in `neo-front-input-worker.ts`, which today overwrites the owner, refuses to change an owner that is already set; and `work_on` calls `claim(…, 'neo')` for its inputs at hand-off. A final reply still claims its inputs as today. So once handed off, a later front final is refused and only the full session answers. The guard and the settlement below read the stored `owner` column only.
   **Hand-off settles as handed off.** `settleConvoInput` (`front-outcome.ts`) today returns "answered" as soon as any stored owner is set. It keeps that for owner `front`; for owner `neo` it returns handed off, and answering is the full session's job from then on. No new failure reply here, by the human's decision: until redelivery and a retry control for the human land as a later change, a handed-off input whose full-session turn ends without an answer gets no daemon notice.
3. **The front catches up on its next input.** When it builds the front's next human input (`front-job.ts`), it puts each part in its own tag so a small model cannot mix them up: `<already-said>` holds each newer publication from the full session, oldest first, as `<said replyTo="…">full text</said>`; then the human's new message in `<human-message id="…">`. `front.md` gets one line: "`<already-said>` is what you already told the human since your last turn. It is for you; do not repeat it unless asked. The human's new words are only in `<human-message>`." The tag names join `RESERVED_TAGS` in `session-envelope.ts`, so text from the human or a publication cannot fake them (text over 4 KiB is cut, ending "… (shown in full to the human)"). The window is by publication sequence, not time: when the daemon builds a front input it records the highest publication sequence it included, in a new nullable column `catch_up_through` on that input's `neo_front_inputs` row (a new migration, and the column added to the table's DDL in `m496-neo-front-inputs.ts`, which `createNeoTables` in `schema/neo.ts` re-runs for fresh and test databases). The next input includes publications above the `catch_up_through` of the latest front input that reached `consumed`. A publication that lands while an input is in flight is above that number and shows next time; an input that failed or fell back never reaches `consumed`, so its publications show again. No extra model call and no extra speech; the front just knows what was said before it answers.
4. **Hand-off is the front's own action.** Rename `run_in_background` to `work_on` and describe it as something the front does itself: "For anything you cannot answer from your reads (check, search, change or run), call this with the human message IDs. It takes a while. When it is done, the answer goes to the human by itself, and you will see it in `<already-said>` on your next turn." The prompt also says: "Never name your tools to the human."  Its only inputs are `requestId` and `messageIds`; the free-text request is removed, so nothing is restated. It returns `{"started": true}`. The full session receives only the recorded human messages, exactly as in single mode; the wake note ("Convo is waiting … message.send … only Convo publishes") and the front's text are deleted. The per-input text the daemon gives the front (`front-job.ts`) names `work_on` instead of `run_in_background`. The prompt never says background, result, another session or hand-off. `handoff_read` is removed from the front: with the send route gone no references reach it, and the full session publishes large results itself.
5. **Remove editing a sent message.** About 1% of publications were ever edited, and the human cannot see which one changed. Delete `neo.publication.edit` (`publication-change-operation.ts`, `neo-publication-edit-writes.ts`, its tool prompt `neo.publication.edit.md`, operation name and discovery entry), `NeoPublicationRepository.edit()` and the refusals only it used (`already_edited`, `already_corrected`, `edit_unchanged`), the `publicationId` correction parameter on the front's reply tools, and the "correct a specific published message in place" sentence in `front.md`. A correction is a new message. Stored rows keep their existing revisions and show as today; nothing is deleted. The edit journey (`neo-publication-edit-db`) and edit tests go with it, and the edit steps in `card-reply-daemon.ts` and `publication-open-target.ts` are dropped from those journeys. The verify seeds that call `edit()` directly (`preview-candidates.ts`, `preview-resolve.ts`, `wake-conversation-disclosure-seed.ts`) append a new publication instead.
6. **Remove the old path.** Delete the full session → front `message.send` route, `BACKGROUND_RESULT_NOTE` and its envelope, and the `dual_model_front_required` refusal. The envelope for messages from other sessions stays. Sweep the names the human sees or the front reads: `NEO_REFUSALS` (`front_ack_required` names the old tool; `front_not_speaking` and `front_model_required` say Convo) the failure text in `front-outcome.ts` ("Convo could not complete this reply"), `publication_superseded` ("Edit the existing answer …", which the front now gets when the owner guard refuses it; it becomes "This input already has an answer; send a new message if needed"), the front tool texts in `front-model.ts` that say "background result", the dual-model setting text in `en.ts` and `zh-Hans.ts` (and its `i18n.test.ts` assertion) and labels in `ModelsSettings.tsx`, which become "Fast replies: a quick model answers first and Neo does the work", and the session title `Convo` in `folder.ts` and `CONVO_TITLE` in `front-session.ts` (written at creation and on every input), which becomes `Neo fast replies`.

Tests: in dual mode the full session publishes and speaks directly; progress replies do not claim; the front cannot publish a final for an input the full session owns; a handed-off input settles as handed off with no extra message; the front's next input starts with everything said since its last consumed input, and again after a failed delivery; a reply that mentions an answered card produces no re-confirmation; switching modes changes no prompt text.
